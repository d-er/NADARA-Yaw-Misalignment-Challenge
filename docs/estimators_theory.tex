\begin{abstract}
This note is the mathematical companion of the pipeline in \code{src/yaw/}. It
describes every operation that is performed on the data, in the order in which
the scripts perform it, with the exact thresholds that the code uses, and it
derives the estimators from a single sensor model and a single aerodynamic law.
Section~\ref{sec:notation} fixes the notation once; no symbol is introduced later
without being defined where it first appears. Sections~\ref{sec:step1}
to~\ref{sec:step4} follow the four pipeline steps (reconstruction and cleaning,
consensus and states, physics estimators, submission), \S\ref{sec:scoring}
states the scoring rule, and \S\ref{sec:summary} collects the assumptions of
every estimator in one table. Measured outcomes are quoted where they decide
between competing models; the step reports \code{docs/01}--\code{docs/04}
contain the full tables and figures.
\end{abstract}

\section{Notation}\label{sec:notation}

\subsection{Conventions}
All directions are compass bearings in degrees, clockwise from north. A difference
of two bearings is always wrapped to $[-180\dg,180\dg)$,
\begin{equation}
  \wrap(x) = \big((x+180) \bmod 360\big) - 180 .
  \label{eq:wrap}
\end{equation}
Angles enter trigonometric functions in radians; the conversion constant is
written $\rho = \pi/180$ wherever it matters.

Time has three resolutions. The raw stream is sampled at roughly $12$\,s; a raw
sample is indexed by $t$. Minute aggregates are indexed by $m$ (1-minute bins) or
$m_{10}$ (10-minute bins). Calendar days are indexed by $d$. Turbines are indexed
by $i$ (the target) and $j$ (a neighbour). A contiguous run of days with constant
behaviour is a \emph{state}, indexed by $\ell$.

For a set of bearings $x_1,\dots,x_n$ the \emph{circular mean} and the
\emph{mean resultant length} are
\begin{equation}
  \bar x = \operatorname{atan2}\Big(\tfrac1n\sum_i \sin x_i,\ \tfrac1n\sum_i \cos x_i\Big),
  \qquad
  \bar R = \Big|\tfrac1n\sum_i e^{\mathrm{i}x_i}\Big| \in [0,1],
  \label{eq:circmean}
\end{equation}
where $\bar R = 1$ means all bearings are equal and $\bar R\to0$ means they are
spread around the circle. The \emph{circular median} is the ordinary median of the
wrapped deviations from the circular mean,
\begin{equation}
  \cmed(x) = \bar x + \med_i \wrap(x_i - \bar x) .
  \label{eq:circmed}
\end{equation}
It inherits the robustness of the median and does not depend on where the
$\pm180\dg$ cut sits. $\med$ without subscript is the ordinary median.

\section{Step 1: reconstruction, cleaning and aggregation}\label{sec:step1}
\begin{sloppypar}
\emph{Code: \code{yaw.io}, \code{yaw.aggregate}, \code{yaw.quality},
\code{yaw.labels}, \code{yaw.geometry}; script \code{scripts/build\_cache.py};
report \code{docs/01}.}
\end{sloppypar}

\subsection{The change-based stream}
The parquet files hold one row per turbine and timestamp with seven channels
($P$, $U$, $\Omega$, $\Omega_g$, $\varphi$, $\Nr$, $\Wr$). The stream is
\emph{change-based}: a channel is written only when its value changed, and a
null means ``unchanged since the previous row'', not ``missing''. Per channel
the fraction of written rows is $0.5$--$0.8$, except $\Nr$, which is written on
only $\approx6\,\%$ of rows because the nacelle moves only during yaw
manoeuvres.

\paragraph{Reconstruction rule.} Rows are sorted by (turbine, $t$). Before
filling, the indicator
\begin{equation}
  u_t = \mathbf 1[\Nr \text{ written at } t]
  \label{eq:nac_update}
\end{equation}
is kept: it marks a \emph{yaw controller action} and is lost after filling.
Then every channel is forward-filled per turbine (\code{ffill}), so that each
row carries the last known value of every channel. Nothing is interpolated;
a value holds until the next write.

\paragraph{The vane.} The vane angle is not shipped. In this SCADA the wind
direction field is computed by the controller as heading plus vane, so
\begin{equation}
  v_t = \wrap(\Wr - \Nr)_t
  \label{eq:vane}
\end{equation}
returns the vane reading exactly (checked on the raw pairs in \code{docs/01}).
Consequently $\Wr$ carries whatever offset the nacelle encoder carries, and only
$v$ is encoder-free. This single fact shapes the whole pipeline
(\S\ref{sec:sensor}).

\paragraph{Asynchronous writes (a trap).} Because $\Nr$ and $\Wr$ are written
independently, at a row where $\Nr$ was just updated the filled $\Wr$ still holds
the value computed at the \emph{previous} $\Wr$ write. Equation~\eqref{eq:vane}
then reads $v^{\mathrm{old}} - \Delta$ (with $\Delta$ the heading change) until
$\Wr$ refreshes, whatever the physical vane does. Nothing at the 12-second
scale may therefore use $v$ across a heading update; see \S\ref{sec:c5}.

\subsection{Aggregation with circular statistics}\label{sec:agg}
The filled stream is binned to 1-minute and 10-minute bins. For a bin containing
raw samples $t\in m$:
\begin{itemize}
\item linear channels ($P,U,\Omega,\Omega_g,\varphi,v$): the mean $\bar P_m$, etc.;
      in addition the within-bin standard deviation of $P$, $U$ and $v$, and the
      median $v^{\med}_m$ of the vane;
\item directions ($\Nr,\Wr$): the circular mean \eqref{eq:circmean} and the
      resultant length $\bar R_m$; a naive mean of $350\dg$ and $10\dg$ would be
      $180\dg$, and several turbines spend much time near north;
\item counts: number of raw rows $n_m$, number of controller actions
      $\sum_{t\in m} u_t$, and the operating fraction
      $\omega_m = \frac1{n_m}\sum_{t\in m}\mathbf 1[P_t > P_{\min}]$.
\end{itemize}
Per turbine this gives $\approx1.0\times10^6$ minutes from $4.7$--$5.9\times10^6$
raw rows over the $727$--$731$ days of record.

\subsection{Operating regimes}\label{sec:regimes}
The undisclosed scale factor forbids physical thresholds; the thresholds are
therefore set from the data. The $99.9$th percentile of $P$ defines
$P_{\mathrm{rated}} = 4800$. A minute is
\begin{align}
  \text{operating}    &\iff \bar P_m > P_{\min} = 100 \quad(\approx2\,\% \text{ of rated}),\\
  \text{partial load} &\iff \text{operating} \ \wedge\ \bar P_m < 0.85\,P_{\mathrm{rated}} \ \wedge\ \bar\varphi_m < 0.5\dg .
  \label{eq:regime}
\end{align}
Pitch is $-3\ldots-1.7\dg$ below rated and rises above it, so the pitch condition
removes the above-rated region cleanly. Above rated the controller holds power
constant by pitching and the power carries no yaw information; every
power-based estimator therefore works in partial load only. The estimators of
\S\ref{sec:step3} use a tighter version, \eqref{eq:good}.

\subsection{Daily table and sensor-health flags}\label{sec:health}
Per turbine and day the minutes are summarised: number of minutes, rows and
controller actions, operating and partial-load minutes, and, over the
\emph{operating} minutes only, the daily median vane
\begin{equation}
  v^{\med}_d = \med_{m\in d,\ \text{operating}} v^{\med}_m ,
  \label{eq:vane_daily}
\end{equation}
its inter-quartile range, the mean within-minute vane standard deviation, the
median pitch and rotor speed, and the circular mean heading. Three flags mark
days that no estimator may use:
\begin{align*}
  \text{frozen vane} &: \text{mean within-minute vane std} < 0.05\dg \ \wedge\ \text{operating minutes} > 60,\\
  \text{no yaw}      &: \textstyle\sum_d u_t = 0 \ \wedge\ \text{operating minutes} > 60,\\
  \text{sparse}      &: \text{raw rows} < 1000 .
\end{align*}
A day is \emph{healthy} when none of the three is set. The flags catch two real
faults: \code{PPP\_WTG13} reports $v=-34.0\dg$ with zero variance and no
controller action on 8--16 Feb 2024 and $v=+39.1\dg$ on 9--12 Oct 2024 (11 and 7
days); no other turbine has a frozen vane. Sparse days are $1$--$8$ per turbine.

\subsection{Labels and label-derived states}\label{sec:labels}
The label $\theta^\star_d$ is given per row of the training turbines and is
constant within a day; the daily series is its first value per day
($195$, $312$ and $195$ labelled days on \code{PPP\_WTG12/13/14}, January to
November 2023). The organisers score only days outside a transition window
around each label step, but do not publish the window rule; it is reconstructed
as follows. The daily label series of each turbine is segmented into
piecewise-constant states with the PELT algorithm (\S\ref{sec:pelt}) under an
$L_2$ cost with penalty $\lambda=20$ and minimum length $n_{\min}=7$ days. The
state level is the median label within the state. A day is a \emph{transition}
when it lies within $7$ calendar days of a state boundary (gaps count as days)
or when its label deviates by more than $0.75\dg$ from its state level; a day is
\emph{scored} when it is labelled and not a transition. This gives $2$, $6$ and
$2$ states and $180$, $217$ and $180$ scored days. All local scoring
(\S\ref{sec:scoring}) uses this table; the rule lives in one function
(\code{yaw.labels.add\_states}) so it can be changed in one place.

\subsection{Layout, neighbours and wake sectors}\label{sec:wake}
The layout files give coordinates with an unknown common scale, so distances are
only used as ratios and bearings are exact. For turbines $i,j$ on the same site
with coordinate differences $(\Delta x,\Delta y)$,
\begin{equation}
  \phi_{ij} = \operatorname{atan2}(\Delta x, \Delta y) \bmod 360
  \label{eq:bearing}
\end{equation}
is the bearing from $i$ to $j$. The neighbours of $i$ are the same-site turbines
sorted by distance. A minute of turbine $i$ is \emph{waked} when its
(de-stepped, \S\ref{sec:destep}) wind direction lies within $\pm20\dg$ of
$\phi_{ij}$ for any published neighbour $j$ closer than four times the distance
of the nearest published neighbour. Only $16$ of the site's turbines are
published, so the mask is a lower bound on real waking; residual partial wakes
are one of the sector-dependent nuisances of Lemma~\ref{lem:tilt}.

\subsection{Pair offsets}
For every same-site pair $(i,j)$ and every 10-minute bin in which both turbines
operate more than $90\,\%$ of the bin ($\omega_{m_{10}}>0.9$ for both), the
heading difference $D_{ij}=\wrap(\Nr^i-\Nr^j)$ and the wind-direction
difference $\wrap(\Wr^i-\Wr^j)$ are formed, and their daily medians and
inter-quartile ranges are stored ($65$ pairs). This table is diagnostic
(\code{figures/01\_vane\_and\_offsets.png}); the consensus of \S\ref{sec:step2}
recomputes the pair series with a circular median.

\subsection{What the cleaning revealed}
Three facts from this step drive the design:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The vane set-point is not zero.} On every PPP turbine the daily
      median vane in operation is $-2.7\ldots-3.7\dg$; on SSS it is $-0.8$ or
      $-3.1\dg$, and \code{SSS\_WTG06} wanders between $0$ and $-4\dg$ over
      weeks. The controller nulls the vane at $\approx-3\dg$, not at $0$.
\item \textbf{Encoders lose north.} \code{PPP\_WTG12}'s heading jumps by
      $\approx138\dg$ against every neighbour from August 2023 while its label does
      not move; on $10$--$17\,\%$ of days a turbine of the WTG12/13/14 cluster sits
      $100$--$180\dg$ away from all neighbours for several days while producing
      normally. These are encoder-frame events, not flow.
\item \textbf{Labels and vane can move in opposite directions.} On
      \code{PPP\_WTG13} the vane median moved from $\approx-0.5$ to $\approx-3\dg$
      on 14--18 January 2023 while the label went from $-11.5$ to $-8.1\dg$. This
      becomes the sign check of \S\ref{sec:signcheck}.
\end{enumerate}

\section{The sensor model}\label{sec:sensor}

\subsection{True and reported quantities}
At any instant the unobserved quantities are the true wind direction $\Wt$, the
true nacelle heading $\Nt$ and the yaw error $\gamma=\wrap(\Wt-\Nt)$. The stream
reports
\begin{align}
  \Nr &= \Nt + \delta_n, \label{eq:Nr}\\
  v   &= g\,\gamma + \delta_v, \label{eq:v}\\
  \Wr &= \Nr + v . \label{eq:Wr}
\end{align}
Equation~\eqref{eq:Nr} says the encoder has no north reference: $\delta_n$ is an
arbitrary constant that changes at every re-reference. Equation~\eqref{eq:v}
says the vane measures the yaw error with a mounting offset $\delta_v$ and a gain
$g\le1$; the gain models the well-documented damping of the inflow angle behind a
rotating rotor (wake swirl, nacelle body).\footnote{Everything below is written
for general $g$; the implementation uses $g=1$ because \S\ref{sec:c5} shows $g$
cannot be measured from this data.} Equation~\eqref{eq:Wr} is not an
assumption but the encoding of the data set, \S\ref{sec:step1}.

\subsection{The controller and the definition of the label}
The yaw controller filters $v$ over minutes and rotates the nacelle whenever the
filtered reading leaves a deadband (measured occupancy $\approx\pm8\dg$) around
its set-point $s$. In steady state the controller therefore enforces
$\E[v]=s$, and from \eqref{eq:v}
\begin{equation}
  \theta \;\equiv\; \E[\gamma] \;=\; \frac{s-\delta_v}{g}.
  \label{eq:theta_def}
\end{equation}
$\theta$ is the \emph{static yaw misalignment}: the persistent yaw error the
controller cannot see because it sits inside its own measurement. The WindFit
reference system measures it with an independent sensor and fits the
power-optimal heading over several days, which is why its daily series is
smoothed and drifts for about a week before each step, and why days near a step
are not scored. The label is negative on all three training turbines; the
sign check of \S\ref{sec:signcheck} establishes that the label sign coincides
with $\gamma$ as defined here.

Two consequences shape everything that follows.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The vane cannot see $\theta$.} Averaged over any window the vane
      reads $s$ whatever $\theta$ is. Measured: $v^{\med}_d\approx-3\dg$ on
      every PPP turbine irrespective of the label (correlation with the label
      $-0.33$). Information about $\theta$ must come from the \emph{response} of
      the turbine to $\gamma$ (power) or from an external direction reference
      (neighbours).
\item \textbf{$\theta$ changes in three ways} that the sensors do not treat
      alike: a change of the set-point $s$, of the vane offset $\delta_v$, or of
      the gain $g$. All three move $\theta$ by \eqref{eq:theta_def}; only the
      first two move the neighbour residual of \S\ref{sec:step2}; and an encoder
      re-reference ($\delta_n$) moves the residual without moving $\theta$ at
      all.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Aerodynamic model of power under yaw}\label{sec:aero}
Actuator-disc theory for a rotor yawed by $\gamma$ replaces the free-stream speed
$U_\infty$ by its axial component $U_\infty\cos\gamma$ in the mass flow and in
the extracted energy, which gives $P(\gamma)=P(0)\cos^3\gamma$. Real rotors show
a smaller exponent because the disc also captures part of the tangential
component and because the power coefficient shifts with $\gamma$ under torque
control. The standard empirical form, also assumed by the OpenOA static-yaw
method, is
\begin{equation}
  P(U_\infty,\gamma) = P_0(U_\infty)\,\cos^k\gamma ,
  \qquad k\in[1.4,\,3],
  \label{eq:cosk}
\end{equation}
with $P_0$ the power of the aligned rotor. The implementation fixes $k=2$ after
finding that a free $k$ wanders between $0.2$ and $13$ from month to month
(\S\ref{sec:c1_bias}); with $k$ fixed the only unknown of the curve is its
location.

\paragraph{Small angles and the deadband.} With $\gamma$ in radians,
\begin{equation}
  \cos^k\gamma = 1 - \frac{k}{2}\gamma^2 + O(\gamma^4),
  \qquad
  \ln\cos^k\gamma = -\frac{k}{2}\gamma^2 - \frac{k}{12}\gamma^4 + O(\gamma^6).
  \label{eq:cosk_small}
\end{equation}
Over the $\pm8\dg$ deadband, with $k=2$, the power varies by
$\tfrac{k}{2}(8\rho)^2 \approx 2\,\%$. Every estimator that locates the peak of
\eqref{eq:cosk} from operational data must therefore resolve a curvature whose
full effect is two per cent of power, against sector-, turbulence- and
season-dependent power variations of the same order. This is the root cause of
the failures of C1 and C2.

\paragraph{The nacelle anemometer.} The anemometer sits behind the rotor and
reads $U = U_\infty\, h(\gamma)$ with $h(0)<1$ and $\partial_\gamma h\ne0$: the
rotor-induced deficit itself depends on the yaw angle. Binning by $U$ is
therefore not binning by $U_\infty$; the apparent power--yaw curve at fixed $U$
is $\cos^k\gamma/h^3(\gamma)$. Since $h$ is even in $\gamma$ to leading order
this does not move the peak, but it flattens or sharpens the curve, i.e.\ it is
absorbed into an effective $k$: another reason to fix $k$.

\section{Step 2: encoder de-stepping, consensus residual and states}\label{sec:step2}
\begin{sloppypar}
\emph{Code: \code{yaw.consensus}; script \code{scripts/segment\_states.py};
report \code{docs/02}.}
\end{sloppypar}

\subsection{Sensor algebra of the residual}\label{sec:algebra}
Suppose a consensus estimate $c(t)$ of the true wind direction is available from
the neighbours, of the form $c = \Wt + \kappa$ with $\kappa$ a constant that
depends on the neighbours' own offsets. From \eqref{eq:Nr} and
$\gamma=\Wt-\Nt$,
\begin{equation}
  \wrap(\Nr - c) = \Nt + \delta_n - \Wt - \kappa = -\gamma + \delta_n - \kappa .
  \label{eq:residual}
\end{equation}
Averaged over a day, $\E[\gamma]=\theta$, so the daily residual obeys
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\ r(d) = -\theta(d) + \delta_n(d) - \kappa\ }
  \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
  \Delta r = -\Delta\theta \quad\text{whenever } \Delta\delta_n = 0 .
  \label{eq:dr}
\end{equation}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:residual}
The residual identifies \emph{changes} of the static misalignment in physical
degrees with slope exactly $-1$, independent of the vane ($g$, $\delta_v$) and
of the aerodynamics; its \emph{level} is not identifiable, because $\delta_n$
and $\kappa$ are unknown constants.
\end{proposition}
\noindent This is the ``consensus anchor'' of the leading team. Using $\Wr$
instead of $\Nr$ gives $\Wr - c = \delta_n + \delta_v + (g-1)\gamma - \kappa$,
a constant for $g=1$ and a damped version of \eqref{eq:dr} for $g<1$; the
implementation uses $\Nr$. The residual is blind to changes of $\theta$ that
occur through $g$ (the wind side of $\gamma$ does not move), but such changes
are also invisible to the label mechanism to first order.

The consensus $c$ is never formed explicitly. Instead the residual is built pair
by pair, \S\ref{sec:cons_build}, which is equivalent because every pair
difference $D_{ij} = \wrap(\Nr^i-\Nr^j)$ contains $-\gamma^i+\gamma^j+\delta_n^i-\delta_n^j$
and the neighbour's terms are constant over a day up to noise.

\subsection{Pair series and encoder re-references}\label{sec:destep}
$\delta_n$ is piecewise constant with jumps at re-references (service, power
cycle of the encoder). A jump of $\delta_n^i$ moves $D_{ij}$ against \emph{all}
neighbours $j$ by the same amount on the same day; the detection rule uses
exactly this signature.

\paragraph{Reference set.} For each turbine $i$ the $n_{\mathrm{nb}}=4$ nearest
same-site turbines are its references, excluding \code{PPP\_WTG12} and
\code{PPP\_WTG16}, whose headings are too unstable to serve as references (they
still receive their own residual from the remaining neighbours).

\paragraph{Daily pair series.} From the 10-minute aggregates, for bins where both
turbines operate more than $90\,\%$ of the time,
\begin{equation}
  D_{ij}(d) = \cmed_{m_{10}\in d}\ \wrap\big(\Nr^i - \Nr^j\big)_{m_{10}} .
  \label{eq:pair_daily}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Level shifts.} Each daily pair series is unwrapped (so that a
crossing of $\pm180\dg$ is not read as a jump) and segmented with PELT under an
$L_1$ cost, penalty $\lambda=150$, minimum length $n_{\min}=10$ days
(\S\ref{sec:pelt}). The $L_1$ cost (median per segment) is chosen so that single
outlying days do not create segments. Each boundary yields a candidate jump
$J$, the wrapped difference of the medians of the adjacent segments.

\paragraph{Attribution rule.} A candidate jump on date $d$ in the series
$(i,j)$ is credited to the encoder of $i$ when candidates with $|J|\ge25\dg$ of
the same sign appear within $\pm3$ days in at least $60\,\%$ of $i$'s pairs and
in at least two of them. The credited jump is the median of the matching
candidates and its date the earliest of them. Shifts below $25\dg$ cannot be
attributed by majority: a $5\dg$ misalignment change and a $5\dg$
re-reference look identical in every pair. That is why the state segmentation
later types boundaries with a jump above $15\dg$ as encoder events
(\S\ref{sec:states}) and the submission refuses to carry $\Delta r$ across
them.

\paragraph{De-stepping.} With credited jumps $(d_1,J_1),(d_2,J_2),\dots$ of
turbine $i$, both direction channels are corrected from each date on,
\begin{equation}
  \Nr^{i}(t) \leftarrow \Big(\Nr^{i}(t) - \sum_{n:\ d_n\le t} J_n\Big) \bmod 360,
  \qquad \text{and likewise } \Wr^{i} .
  \label{eq:destep}
\end{equation}
$\Wr$ must be corrected too because it carries $\delta_n$ by \eqref{eq:Wr}; $v$
is unaffected. Sixty-seven steps on ten turbines were credited, none on
\code{PPP\_WTG17}.

\subsection{Building the residual}\label{sec:cons_build}
After de-stepping, three cleaning operations and one correction are applied
to each pair before the residual is formed.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Excursion masking.} The daily pair series \eqref{eq:pair_daily}
      is recomputed on the de-stepped headings and compared with its centred
      31-day rolling median (at least 7 days present). Days with
      $|\wrap(D_{ij}(d) - \text{rolling median})| > 30\dg$ are dropped from the
      pair. These are the multi-day frame excursions shorter than the
      $n_{\min}=10$ days of the step detector.
\item \textbf{Sector correction.} Terrain steering and partial wakes make the
      direction difference between two sites a function of the wind direction.
      With $\sigma_j$ the $30\dg$ sector of the neighbour's (de-stepped) wind
      direction, $\sigma_j = \lfloor \Wr^j / 30\dg\rfloor$, the sector median
      $m_{ij}(\sigma_j) = \cmed\{D_{ij}(m_{10}) : \text{bins in sector } \sigma_j\}$
      is subtracted bin by bin,
      \begin{equation}
        e_{ij}(m_{10}) = \wrap\big(D_{ij}(m_{10}) - m_{ij}(\sigma_j(m_{10}))\big) .
        \label{eq:sector}
      \end{equation}
      The neighbour's wind direction is used as the sector variable because it
      is not affected by the target's own yaw error.
\item \textbf{Daily pair residual.} $e_{ij}(d) = \med_{m_{10}\in d} e_{ij}(m_{10})$
      over days with at least $12$ valid bins; its daily inter-quartile range is
      kept as a noise measure.
\item \textbf{Noisy references.} A pair whose median daily inter-quartile range
      exceeds twice the median of the turbine's pairs is dropped.
\end{enumerate}
The daily residual of turbine $i$ is the median over the surviving pairs,
\begin{equation}
  r_i(d) = \med_{j}\ e_{ij}(d) .
  \label{eq:r_def}
\end{equation}
The double median (over bins, then over neighbours) makes \eqref{eq:r_def}
robust to one bad neighbour and to a few bad bins; the measured day-to-day
noise is $\approx2\dg$ (median absolute deviation). The price is that changes
below $\approx1\dg$, the two small label steps on WTG12 and WTG14, are under the
noise floor. In terms of \S\ref{sec:algebra}, the sector medians $m_{ij}$
absorb $\delta_n^j$, $\theta^j$ and the terrain term into the constant $\kappa$,
and $r_i$ is \eqref{eq:dr} up to that constant.

\subsection{Change-point detection: PELT}\label{sec:pelt}
Both the label states (\S\ref{sec:labels}), the encoder steps
(\S\ref{sec:destep}) and the SCADA states (\S\ref{sec:states}) use the same
algorithm. Given a series $y_1,\dots,y_T$, a partition into contiguous segments
$\mathcal S_1,\dots,\mathcal S_M$ is chosen to minimise
\begin{equation}
  \sum_{\ell=1}^{M}\ C(\mathcal S_\ell) \;+\; \lambda\,(M-1),
  \qquad |\mathcal S_\ell| \ge n_{\min},
  \label{eq:pelt}
\end{equation}
with the segment cost either
\begin{equation}
  C_{L_2}(\mathcal S) = \sum_{d\in\mathcal S}\big(y_d - \bar y_{\mathcal S}\big)^2
  \qquad\text{or}\qquad
  C_{L_1}(\mathcal S) = \sum_{d\in\mathcal S}\big|y_d - \med(y_{\mathcal S})\big| .
\end{equation}
The pruned exact linear time algorithm (PELT) solves \eqref{eq:pelt} exactly by
dynamic programming over the last change point, discarding candidates that can
never become optimal. The $L_2$ cost is the negative log-likelihood of a
piecewise-constant Gaussian mean up to scale, so $\lambda$ acts as a BIC-type
penalty: a break is accepted when it lowers the residual sum of squares by more
than $\lambda$. For a level jump $J$ over $n$ days on both sides, the reduction
is $nJ^2/2$, so with $\lambda=400$ a jump is detected when $nJ^2/2\gtrsim400$,
e.g.\ $J\ge2.4\dg$ over $140$ days or $J\ge5\dg$ over $30$ days. The penalties
were chosen on the training turbines: lower values split
\code{PPP\_WTG17}'s 2023 into up to $13$ states and \code{SSS\_WTG06} into $50$,
noise rather than physics.

\subsection{SCADA states}\label{sec:states}
The residual is put on a complete daily calendar, smoothed with a centred 7-day
rolling median (at least 3 days present) and linearly interpolated across gaps so
that states are contiguous in calendar time,
\begin{equation}
  \tilde r_i(d) = \med\{r_i(d') : |d'-d|\le3\} .
\end{equation}
PELT with the $L_2$ cost, $\lambda=400$ and $n_{\min}=14$ days is run on
$\tilde r_i$. Each state $\ell$ receives the level
\begin{equation}
  \bar r_\ell = \med_{d\in\mathcal S_\ell} \tilde r_i(d) .
  \label{eq:rlevel}
\end{equation}
The first day of each state is typed \emph{encoder} when
$|\bar r_\ell-\bar r_{\ell-1}|>15\dg$ (too large for a misalignment change) and
\emph{candidate} otherwise; days within $\pm7$ of a boundary are flagged as
transitions, mirroring the scoring rule of \S\ref{sec:labels}.

Measured on the training turbines against the label-derived states of
\S\ref{sec:labels}: adjusted Rand index $0.47$ / $0.37$ / $0.79$ on
WTG12/13/14, limited by the sub-degree label steps that $r$ cannot see. After
de-stepping, $r$ tracks the inverted label at WTG13's two large events
(20 January and 27 July 2023); at the July event $\Delta r\approx-9\dg$ for a
label step of $+3.65\dg$, i.e.\ the service visit also re-referenced the encoder
by a few degrees. \emph{A small re-reference and a misalignment change are
indistinguishable in $r$}: $r$ gives boundaries reliably and magnitudes only as
a prior. On \code{PPP\_WTG17} the result is two states with the boundary at
2024-01-06 and $\Delta r=-8.05\dg$, i.e.\ $\Delta\theta=+8\dg$ by
\eqref{eq:dr}; on \code{SSS\_WTG06}, $13$ states with three encoder-typed
boundaries.

\section{Step 3: physics estimators of the level}\label{sec:step3}
\begin{sloppypar}
\emph{Code: \code{yaw.estimators}; script \code{scripts/estimate\_angles.py};
report \code{docs/03}.} The residual gives changes; the level must come from
the turbine's response to $\gamma$. Four estimators were derived; none survived
as a level estimator, and the derivations show why.
\end{sloppypar}

\subsection{Common data selection}\label{sec:good}
All estimators of this step use the 1-minute aggregates with the de-stepped
wind direction. A minute of the target is \emph{good} when
\begin{equation}
  300 < \bar P_m < 3600,\qquad \bar\varphi_m < 0.5\dg,\qquad \omega_m > 0.99,\qquad
  \text{unwaked (\S\ref{sec:wake})},\qquad \text{healthy day (\S\ref{sec:health})}.
  \label{eq:good}
\end{equation}
The power window is narrower than \eqref{eq:regime}: it excludes start-up and
the knee below rated, where the $\cos^k$ law and the torque law both bend. When
a reference neighbour is involved (C2, C4) the reference's minute must satisfy
the first four conditions as well; the reference is chosen among the three
nearest allowed neighbours as the one with the largest number of jointly good
minutes.

Estimates are computed per \emph{window}: each label state (scored days), each
SCADA state (non-transition days), each calendar quarter, and the whole record.

\subsection{C1: power-versus-vane peak with the nacelle anemometer}\label{sec:c1}

\paragraph{Estimator.} Take good minutes with $|\bar v_m|<20\dg$ and a
within-minute vane standard deviation below $6\dg$, so that the minute has a
well-defined yaw angle. The free-stream speed is unknown, so it is removed by
normalisation within wind-speed bins: with $B(U)$ the $0.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$ bin of
$\bar U_m$ (bins with fewer than $30$ minutes are dropped),
\begin{equation}
  \tilde P_m = \frac{\bar P_m}{\med\{\bar P_{m'} : \bar U_{m'} \in B(\bar U_m)\}} .
  \label{eq:norm}
\end{equation}
By \eqref{eq:cosk}, and because the bin median is taken over all yaw angles in
the bin, which are concentrated near the set-point,
\begin{equation}
  \E[\tilde P \mid \bar v] \;\approx\; A\,\cos^k\!\big(\bar v - \theta_0\big),
  \qquad \theta_0 = \delta_v \ \ (g=1),
  \label{eq:c1_model}
\end{equation}
where the peak location $\theta_0$ is the vane reading at which the rotor is
aligned, $\gamma=0\Leftrightarrow v=\delta_v$ by \eqref{eq:v}. For $g\ne1$ the
curve becomes $\cos^k((\bar v-\delta_v)/g)$: the peak is still at $\delta_v$ and
the curvature is stretched by $1/g^2$, which with fixed $k$ inflates $A$ and
leaves $\theta_0$ unbiased to first order.

The minutes are binned by $\bar v$ in $1\dg$ steps; bin $n$ is represented by
its median $y_n$ and its count $c_n$ (bins with fewer than $10$ minutes are
dropped, and at least $8$ bins are required). The two parameters are the
weighted non-linear least-squares solution
\begin{equation}
  (\hat A,\hat\theta_0) = \arg\min_{A,\theta_0}\ \sum_n c_n\big(A\cos^k(v_n-\theta_0) - y_n\big)^2 ,
  \qquad |\theta_0|\le40\dg,
  \label{eq:c1_ls}
\end{equation}
found with a bounded trust-region method; in the free-$k$ variant $k$ is a third
parameter with $0.2\le k\le15$. The bin median rather than the mean is used
because the within-bin distribution of $\tilde P$ is heavy-tailed (start-up
minutes, curtailment tails the power window does not fully remove).

\paragraph{From the peak to the misalignment.} Combining \eqref{eq:c1_model}
with the controller identity \eqref{eq:theta_def},
\begin{equation}
  \hat\theta = \frac{s-\hat\theta_0}{g} \approx v^{\med} - \hat\theta_0 ,
  \label{eq:c1_theta}
\end{equation}
i.e.\ the misalignment is the distance between where the controller \emph{holds}
the vane and where the power \emph{peaks} on the vane axis. The literature often
reports $-\hat\theta_0$ alone, which silently assumes $s=0$; on this fleet
$s\approx-3\dg$.

\paragraph{Sampling variance.} Linearising the weighted residual vector
$\mathbf e(p)$ of \eqref{eq:c1_ls} at the solution with Jacobian $\mathbf J$
gives the Gauss--Newton covariance
\begin{equation}
  \widehat{\Cov}(\hat p) = \hat\sigma^2 (\mathbf J^\top\mathbf J)^{-1},
  \qquad
  \hat\sigma^2 = \frac{\|\mathbf e(\hat p)\|^2}{n_{\mathrm{bins}} - \dim p},
  \label{eq:gn_cov}
\end{equation}
and the reported standard error is the square root of the $\theta_0$ diagonal
element. It is a conditional error: it assumes the bin medians scatter
independently around a $\cos^k$ curve. The measured quarter-to-quarter scatter
of $\hat\theta_0$ within one label state ($4$--$9\dg$) is five to ten times
larger, which proves that model error dominates.

\subsubsection{Why the peak is biased: the tilt lemma}\label{sec:c1_bias}
\begin{lemma}[Peak shift under a linear nuisance]\label{lem:tilt}
Let the true bin curve be $y(v)=A[1-\tfrac{k}{2}\rho^2(v-\theta_0)^2]$ in
degrees, and let an unmodelled nuisance add a linear trend $\varepsilon\,v$
(normalised power per degree). Then the peak of the observed curve is at
\begin{equation}
  v^\ast = \theta_0 + \frac{\varepsilon}{A\,k\,\rho^2}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$\partial_v\big[y(v)+\varepsilon v\big] = -A k\rho^2 (v-\theta_0) + \varepsilon = 0$.
\end{proof}
With $k=2$, $\rho^2=3.05\times10^{-4}$, $A\approx1$, a tilt of one per cent of
power across the $8\dg$ deadband, $\varepsilon=0.01/8=1.25\times10^{-3}$ per
degree, shifts the peak by $1.25\times10^{-3}/(2\times3.05\times10^{-4})\approx2\dg$.
Any effect that correlates power with the sign of the vane deviation at the
one-per-cent level (a sector with slightly different shear or turbulence, or the
fact that the vane deviates to one side while the wind is veering and veering
periods have their own power statistics) moves the fitted misalignment by
degrees. The measured curves change shape between quarters of the same label
state (monotone rising, monotone falling, peaked; \code{figures/03\_estimators.png}),
which is this lemma at work. The same mechanism makes the free-$k$ fit unstable:
$k$ and the tilt are nearly collinear on a $\pm8\dg$ support. This is the
failure the challenge README warns about (``ranks the three training turbines
in the wrong order'') and the one the leading team's T0 entry reproduces.

\subsection{C2: neighbour power as the wind reference}\label{sec:c2}
C2 keeps \eqref{eq:c1_model}--\eqref{eq:c1_ls} but replaces the wind-speed bin in
\eqref{eq:norm} by a bin of the \emph{simultaneous power of the reference
neighbour}, $\bar P^j_m$, in bins of $150$ power units, using minutes that are
good for both turbines:
\begin{equation}
  \tilde P_m^{\mathrm{C2}} = \frac{\bar P^i_m}{\med\{\bar P^i_{m'} : \bar P^j_{m'} \in B(\bar P^j_m)\}} .
\end{equation}
The motivation is the anemometer deficit $h(\gamma)$ of \S\ref{sec:aero}: the
neighbour's power is a wind reference that does not sit in the target's wake
and is not a function of the target's yaw angle.
\begin{assumption}
(i) The neighbour's free-stream speed is a monotone function of the target's,
stable over the window; (ii) the neighbour's own yaw error is stationary over
the window.
\end{assumption}
\noindent Under these the ratio has the same peak $\theta_0=\delta_v$ and the
derivation of C1 goes through. What C2 removes and what it adds is decided by
Lemma~\ref{lem:tilt}: it removes the yaw dependence of the anemometer, which is
even in $\gamma$ and does not shift the peak anyway, and it adds the neighbour's
sector dependence (the speed map between two sites depends on wind direction,
which correlates with the vane deviation). Empirically the C2 standard errors
are $3$--$6\dg$ and its levels ($-5$, $-18$, $-18\dg$ on the training turbines)
are further from the labels than C1. The neighbour is the right idea for the
\emph{speed} bias and the wrong tool for the \emph{tilt} bias.

\subsection{C3: the rotor-speed method}\label{sec:c3}
C3 was planned and not implemented; the derivation shows why it adds nothing
here. In partial load the generator torque follows the optimal tip-speed law
\begin{equation}
  T_g = K_g\,\Omega^2,\qquad P = T_g\Omega = K_g\,\Omega^3 ,
  \label{eq:torque_law}
\end{equation}
with $K_g$ a constant of the controller. Rotor speed and power are therefore
deterministically linked \emph{independently of the yaw angle}: $\Omega$ against
$P$ is the same curve at every $\gamma$, and ``rotor speed at a given power''
carries no yaw information. Yaw information appears only when $\Omega$ is
compared with an external speed reference: at the equilibrium of
\eqref{eq:torque_law} with the aerodynamic torque, $\Omega\propto U_\infty\cos\gamma$
to leading order, so $\Omega/U = \text{const}\cdot\cos\gamma/h(\gamma)$ peaks at
$\gamma=0$. But this is the C1 estimator with $P^{1/3}$ in place of $P$ and the
same anemometer, and by \eqref{eq:torque_law} it is algebraically the same fit.
The rotor-speed method is useful where the power signal is curtailed or
corrupted and $\Omega$ is not; here the reverse holds.

\subsection{C4: yaw manoeuvres as natural experiments}\label{sec:c4}

\paragraph{Idea.} Every yaw manoeuvre rotates the nacelle by a \emph{known}
angle $\Delta$, and a rotation is a rotation regardless of $\delta_n$,
$\delta_v$ or $g$. The power before and after is measured at two yaw errors that
differ by a known amount, so the pair samples the \emph{derivative} of the
$\cos^k$ curve at a known offset from the operating point. The vane is not used;
the estimate is in physical degrees. This is the only estimator of the pipeline
whose level is, in principle, free of the vane.

\paragraph{Manoeuvres.} From the filled 12-second stream, heading updates
($u_t=1$) with $|\wrap(\Delta\Nr)|>0.05\dg$ are grouped into runs separated by
less than $60$\,s. A run is a manoeuvre with start $t_0$, end $t_1$, number of
updates, duration $t_1-t_0$, and rotation
\begin{equation}
  \Delta = \wrap\big(\Nr(t_1) - \Nr(t_0^-)\big),
\end{equation}
the heading after the last update minus the heading before the first. There are
$\approx10^5$ per turbine (ramps of $\approx0.5\dg/\mathrm{s}$, $2$--$30\dg$).

\paragraph{Events.} A manoeuvre with $2\dg\le|\Delta|\le30\dg$ and duration
$\le300$\,s is an event if the five 1-minute bins from $6$ to $2$ minutes before
$t_0$ and the five bins from $2$ to $6$ minutes after $t_1$ are all good for
target and reference. Let $\ln P^{\pm}$ be the mean of $\ln\bar P_m$ over the
after/before window of the target and $\ln P^{\pm}_j$ the same for the reference.
The event statistic is the double log-ratio
\begin{equation}
  \ln Q \;\equiv\; \big(\ln P^+ - \ln P^-\big) - \big(\ln P^+_j - \ln P^-_j\big) .
  \label{eq:lnQ}
\end{equation}
Between $9$ and $26\times10^3$ events per turbine survive.

\paragraph{Derivation.} Let the manoeuvre rotate the nacelle from $\Nt^-$ to
$\Nt^+=\Nt^-+\Delta$. The controller rotates towards the wind, so $\Delta>0$
means the wind had drifted clockwise of the nacelle. After the manoeuvre the
controller has restored its equilibrium and the yaw error is the static
misalignment, $\gamma^+=\theta$. Before it, the yaw error was $\theta$ plus the
part of the drift that had already accumulated,
\begin{equation}
  \gamma^- = \theta + f\Delta,\qquad 0<f\le1 ,
  \label{eq:gamma_before}
\end{equation}
where $f=1$ if the wind shifted as a step and the nacelle followed, and $f<1$ if
the wind drifted gradually and the ``before'' window already contains part of
the drift. Under \eqref{eq:cosk}, with the free-stream speed change cancelling
between target and reference (both in partial load, both unwaked),
\begin{equation}
  \ln Q = k\big[\ln\cos\theta - \ln\cos(\theta+f\Delta)\big] + \eta .
  \label{eq:lnQ_model}
\end{equation}
Expanding $\ln\cos(\theta+x)=\ln\cos\theta - x\tan\theta - \tfrac{x^2}{2\cos^2\theta}+O(x^3)$
with $x=f\Delta$ in radians,
\begin{equation}
  \ln Q = k f \tan\theta\;\Delta \;+\; \frac{k f^2}{2\cos^2\theta}\,\Delta^2 + O(\Delta^3) + \eta .
  \label{eq:lnQ_expand}
\end{equation}
\begin{proposition}[Sign and level from the manoeuvre slope]\label{prop:c4}
The coefficient of the term linear in $\Delta$ is $\beta_1 = k f\tan\theta$ per
radian. Its sign is the sign of $\theta$ in the convention $\gamma=\Wt-\Nt$,
clockwise positive, and its magnitude gives
\begin{equation}
  \hat\theta = \arctan\frac{\hat\beta_1}{k f} .
  \label{eq:c4_theta}
\end{equation}
The quadratic term is even in $\Delta$ and positive: a manoeuvre in either
direction raises power on average, by more for larger $|\Delta|$. Only the
\emph{asymmetry} between $+\Delta$ and $-\Delta$ carries $\theta$.
\end{proposition}
Intuitively, if $\theta<0$ (rotor pointing clockwise of the wind), a manoeuvre
with $\Delta<0$ started from $\gamma^-=\theta+f\Delta$, further from zero, and
the gain after it is large; a manoeuvre with $\Delta>0$ started closer to zero
or past it, and the gain is small or negative. For $\theta=-5\dg$, $f=1$,
$k=2$: $\Delta=-20\dg$ gives $P^+/P^-=\cos^2(-5\dg)/\cos^2(-25\dg)=1.21$ and
$\Delta=+20\dg$ gives $\cos^2(-5\dg)/\cos^2(15\dg)=1.06$. This asymmetry (large
gain after $-20\dg$, little after $+20\dg$) is what \code{PPP\_WTG17} shows in
2023.

\paragraph{Estimation.} Events with $|\Delta|\le20\dg$ (at least $50$ of them)
enter a robust regression of $\ln Q$ on $\Delta$ with the Huber loss
\begin{equation}
  \ell_M(e) = \begin{cases} \tfrac12 e^2 & |e|\le M \\ M|e| - \tfrac12 M^2 & |e|>M\end{cases},
  \qquad M = 1.35 \text{ (in units of the residual scale)},
  \label{eq:huber}
\end{equation}
because $\ln Q$ has heavy tails (gusts inside a four-minute window). The
quadratic term is not fitted: with $\Delta$ symmetric around zero it is
orthogonal to the linear term and is absorbed by the intercept and the residual.
The slope is fitted per degree and converted to per radian in
\eqref{eq:c4_theta}. Its standard error is the standard deviation of the slope
over $200$ bootstrap resamples of the events, and the standard error of
$\hat\theta$ follows from
\begin{equation}
  \widehat{\mathrm{se}}(\hat\theta) = \tfrac12\big|\hat\theta(\hat\beta_1+\mathrm{se}) - \hat\theta(\hat\beta_1-\mathrm{se})\big| .
\end{equation}

\begin{remark}[Sign in the implementation]
The function \code{manoeuvre\_fit} originally returned the
value $\arctan(-\hat\beta_1/(kf))$, which has the opposite sign to
\eqref{eq:c4_theta}. Under
the conventions of \S\ref{sec:sensor} (both headings clockwise bearings,
$\Delta$ = after minus before) the derivation gives the positive sign; the code,
the cached estimates, figure 03 and \code{docs/03} were corrected on
2026-09-11. With the right sign C4 gives $+1.5$, $-0.4$ and $-3.1\dg$ on the
training turbines (labels $-2$, $-8$, $-2\dg$), and its 2023 to 2024 change
on \code{PPP\_WTG17} is $\approx+5\dg$, the same sign as the residual's
$\Delta\theta=-\Delta r\approx+8\dg$. This does not rescue C4 as a level
estimator.
\end{remark}

\subsubsection{Why C4 is not the clean estimator hoped for}\label{sec:c4_fail}
Two systematic effects, both measured, break Proposition~\ref{prop:c4} on this
data.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A meteorological confounder.} A \emph{null test} applies the
      manoeuvre times of \code{PPP\_WTG17} to the power ratio of two unrelated
      turbines that did not manoeuvre. The slope should be zero; it is
      $-1.0\pm0.2\times10^{-3}$ per degree, a third of the ``real'' slope. The
      sign of $\Delta$ is the sign of the wind-direction change (veering versus
      backing), and veering and backing episodes come with their own power
      evolution (frontal passages, diurnal cycle) that is not identical at two
      sites a few hundred metres apart. The reference normalisation in
      \eqref{eq:lnQ} removes the common part of the speed change, not the
      direction-correlated residual. This term adds to $\beta_1$ directly and
      cannot be separated from $\theta$ without a model per sector.
\item \textbf{The unknown fraction $f$.} The level \eqref{eq:c4_theta} scales
      as $1/f$, and $f$ is neither known nor constant. With gradual drift and a
      $\pm8\dg$ deadband $f$ is plausibly $0.3$--$0.6$, so the raw slope
      underestimates $|\theta|$ by a factor two to three.
\end{enumerate}
Different references disagree by $2$--$4\dg$ and the three training turbines are
not ranked correctly. C4 is kept as a feature (the asymmetry is real and large
on some turbine-years) but not used for levels. The one open route to a
label-free level is to difference the slope against the null distribution per
wind-direction sector and manoeuvre direction.

\subsection{C5: the vane gain, and why it cannot be measured here}\label{sec:c5}
If the vane responded instantaneously with gain $g$, a nacelle rotation $\Delta$
during steady wind would produce a vane jump of $-g\Delta$ (\eqref{eq:v} with
$\gamma\to\gamma-\Delta$), and the thousands of manoeuvres would give $g$
directly, converting all vane-unit estimates into physical degrees. On this data
the derived vane jumps by \emph{exactly} $-\Delta$ at every heading update. The
reason is the asynchronous encoding described in \S\ref{sec:step1}: at a
heading update $\Wr$ still holds $\Nr^{\mathrm{old}}+v^{\mathrm{old}}$, so
\eqref{eq:vane} reads $v^{\mathrm{old}}-\Delta$ until $\Wr$ refreshes, whatever
the vane does. The apparent gain is one by construction, and no quantity derived
from the two direction channels at the 12-second scale can recover $g$. C5 was
dropped and $g=1$ is used throughout.

\section{Step 4: the submission model}\label{sec:step4}
\begin{sloppypar}
\emph{Code: \code{yaw.submission}, \code{yaw.evaluate}; script
\code{scripts/build\_submission.py}; report \code{docs/04}.}
\end{sloppypar}

\subsection{Structure}
For every turbine-day the prediction is
\begin{equation}
  \hat\theta(d) = L + a + b\,x(d),\qquad
  x(d) = \bar r_{\ell(d)} - \alpha_{\mathrm{seg}(\ell(d))},
  \label{eq:submission}
\end{equation}
where $\ell(d)$ is the day's SCADA state (\S\ref{sec:states}),
$\mathrm{seg}(\ell)$ its \emph{encoder-free segment} (a maximal run of states
without an encoder-typed boundary, i.e.\ the cumulative count of encoder
boundaries up to $\ell$), $\alpha$ the segment anchor, $L$ the level prior, and
$(a,b)$ either fixed by physics or fitted on the labelled turbines:
\begin{align*}
  \text{T0 (label-free):}\quad & a=0,\ b=-1 \quad\text{from \eqref{eq:dr}},\\
  \text{T3 (calibrated):}\quad & (a,b) \text{ from \S\ref{sec:t3}}.
\end{align*}
The submitted \code{cluster} field is $\ell(d)$. Days without a SCADA state
(gaps at the record edges) take the nearest state's values; predictions are
rounded to three decimals. The clusters are computed from the residual over
all $731$ days without any knowledge of the template or of which days are
scored; the only calendar prior is $n_{\min}=14$ days.

\subsection{The level prior $L$}
No label-free level estimator survived \S\ref{sec:step3}. The prior is the
\emph{vane set-point}: from \eqref{eq:theta_def} with $g=1$ and the assumption
$\delta_v=0$, $\theta=s$, and $s$ is measured as
\begin{equation}
  L = \med_{d\ \text{healthy}}\ v^{\med}_d ,
  \label{eq:L}
\end{equation}
the median over healthy days of the daily median vane in operation
\eqref{eq:vane_daily}. On the PPP site this is $-2.7$ to $-3.7\dg$ on every
turbine, on SSS $-0.8$ to $-3.3\dg$. The prior is right when the vane is
mounted true and the operator has chosen a non-zero set-point deliberately; it
is wrong by $-\delta_v$ otherwise. On the training turbines it gives biases of
$-0.45$, $+2.5$ and $-0.8\dg$: within a degree on two of three, and $4.5\dg$ off
on WTG13, whose vane evidently carries its own offset.

\subsection{The anchor $\alpha$}
The prior \eqref{eq:L} is a statement about the turbine's \emph{typical} state,
so the state levels must be centred before the slope is applied. Two choices
were evaluated on the training turbines, both taken over the days $d$ of the
segment:
\begin{align}
  \alpha^{\mathrm{med}} &= \med_{d\in\mathrm{seg}} \bar r_{\ell(d)}
  \quad\text{(the level of the state holding most days),}\\
  \alpha^{\mathrm{mean}} &= \frac{1}{|\mathrm{seg}|}\sum_{d\in\mathrm{seg}} \bar r_{\ell(d)}
  \quad\text{(day-weighted average of the state levels).}
\end{align}
The median anchor scores better on train (RMSE $2.18$ versus $2.48$) because the
training turbines have one dominant state. For \code{PPP\_WTG17}, whose two
states hold $370$ and $361$ days, the median anchor is a coin flip that moves the
whole prediction by $4\dg$; the mean anchor minimises the expected squared
error under ignorance of which state is typical, and it was submitted first.
Re-anchoring per segment is what stops re-references below the $25\dg$
attribution threshold of \S\ref{sec:destep} from propagating into the levels.

\subsection{T3 calibration}\label{sec:t3}
On the scored training days the label minus the prior, $y_d=\theta^\star_d-L$,
is regressed on $x_d$ with the Huber loss \eqref{eq:huber},
\begin{equation}
  (\hat a,\hat b) = \arg\min_{a,b} \sum_d \ell_{1.35}\big(y_d - a - b\,x_d\big),
\end{equation}
giving $\hat a=-0.73\dg$, $\hat b=-0.93$ on all three turbines: the labels
confirm the physical slope of $-1$ and shift the level by less than a degree.
Leave-one-turbine-out the fit is \emph{worse} than T0 (RMSE $4.4$ versus $2.5$),
because only WTG13 has label steps large enough to identify $b$; holding it
out collapses $\hat b$ to zero. The T3 file is therefore the T0 file with a
$-0.7\dg$ shift and a $7\,\%$ shrink of the deltas, and the honest reading is
that three labelled turbines cannot calibrate more than the sign.

\subsection{Sign checks}\label{sec:signcheck}
Two independent tests are asserted before any file is written; the script
aborts if either fails.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The least-squares slope of the label on $x$ over the scored training days
      must be negative; measured $-0.74$. This is \eqref{eq:dr} seen from the
      labels.
\item On \code{PPP\_WTG13}, comparing operating samples before 13 January and
      after 19 January 2023, the median vane moved by $-2.6\dg$ while the label
      moved by $+3.0\dg$. By \eqref{eq:theta_def} a change of the set-point (or
      of $\delta_v$ with the controller re-nulling) moves $\theta$ by the same
      amount in the \emph{same} direction, unless the label sign is opposite to
      the raw vane convention. The observed opposite motion therefore fixes the
      label as $-v$ in the raw convention, equivalently as $\gamma=\Wt-\Nt$ once
      the direction in which this controller reports the vane is accounted for.
      The test is asserted as ``vane and label move in opposite directions'' and
      requires $|\Delta v|>1\dg$ and $\Delta\theta^\star>1\dg$.
\end{enumerate}
Together the two tests pin the sign of the submission model, which is all that
matters for scoring.

\section{Scoring rule and what it rewards}\label{sec:scoring}

\subsection{Metrics}
With $\mathcal D$ the set of scored days (\S\ref{sec:labels}),
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{RMSE} = \sqrt{\frac1{|\mathcal D|}\sum_{d\in\mathcal D}(\hat\theta_d-\theta^\star_d)^2},\quad
  \mathrm{MAE} = \frac1{|\mathcal D|}\sum_{d\in\mathcal D}|\hat\theta_d-\theta^\star_d|,\quad
  \mathrm{bias} = \frac1{|\mathcal D|}\sum_{d\in\mathcal D}(\hat\theta_d-\theta^\star_d).
\end{equation}
The tie-breaker is the adjusted Rand index between the submitted clusters and
the label-derived states, averaged over turbines. For a contingency table
$n_{uw}$ (cluster $u$, true state $w$) with margins $n_{u\cdot}$, $n_{\cdot w}$
and $n=|\mathcal D|$,
Write $I=\sum_{uw}\binom{n_{uw}}{2}$ for the number of day pairs that
share both a cluster and a true state, $A=\sum_u\binom{n_{u\cdot}}{2}$ and
$B=\sum_w\binom{n_{\cdot w}}{2}$ for the pairs sharing a cluster and sharing a
state respectively, and $E=AB/\binom n2$ for the value of $I$ expected from a
random partition with the same margins. Then
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{ARI} = \frac{I - E}{\tfrac12(A+B) - E},
\end{equation}
which is $1$ for identical partitions up to relabelling and $0$ in expectation
for a random partition. It is invariant to cluster names; splitting a true state
costs ARI in proportion to the split.

\subsection{Effective sample size and the block bootstrap}
Scored days are runs of a piecewise-constant series, and the error of a
piecewise-constant prediction is constant within each run, so the effective
number of independent observations is the number of states (two on
\code{PPP\_WTG17}, of order fifteen on the training set), not the number of
days. The organisers resample whole states with replacement, score every
submission on the same draw, and call a submission better than another only if
its RMSE is lower on essentially all draws. The local harness reproduces this
with $1000$ draws over (turbine, state) blocks; the resulting $95\,\%$ interval
for the T0 model on the training turbines is $1.8$--$3.3\dg$ around $2.48$,
which is the honest uncertainty of every score quoted in this note.

\subsection{Implication for the strategy}
RMSE differences smaller than that band are ties, and ARI then decides. The
level of $\theta$ is what none of the physics estimators can pin better than a
few degrees, so the winnable quantity is the segmentation (ARI, from
\S\ref{sec:step2}) plus a level that is not badly wrong. The label-free T0 model
scores $2.5\dg$ on the training turbines against a best-constant baseline of
$3.1$--$6.1\dg$; the current T0 leader on the public board scores $3.9\dg$ with a
year-static segmentation.
